CHAPTER 5

Fourier Transform

Fourier series handle periodic signals with a discrete list of frequencies. Let the period go to infinity and the list fills in to a continuous spectrum \(F(\omega)\). That is the Fourier transform, and it works on the whole real line.

Theorem 5.1 · Fourier integral theoremIf \(f\) is absolutely integrable and \(f,f'\) are piecewise continuous, then \[\frac{f(t^-)+f(t^+)}2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty F(\omega)e^{i\omega t}d\omega,\qquad F(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau\] At a jump it gives the midpoint, just as Fourier series do.

5.2 Definition and the transform pair

Definitions 5.3 and 5.4, the convention used in every MA2014 paper

Forward\[F(\omega)=\mathcal F\{f(t)\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt\]
Inverse\[f(t)=\mathcal F^{-1}\{F\}=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty F(\omega)e^{i\omega t}d\omega\]
\(f=u(t)e^{-t}\): \(F=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}e^{-i\omega t}dt=\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+i\omega)t}dt\)
\(=\left[\frac{-e^{-(1+i\omega)t}}{1+i\omega}\right]_0^\infty=\boxed{\frac{1}{1+i\omega}}=\frac{1-i\omega}{1+\omega^2}\)
\(F\) is complex. Its magnitude \(|F|=\frac1{\sqrt{1+\omega^2}}\) says how much of each frequency is present. Its angle is the phase. Pick it in the viewer below.

2025 exam Q2(b) (10 marks)

\(\mathcal F\{f'\}=\int_{-\infty}^\infty f'(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt\). Integrate by parts: \(=\Big[f(t)e^{-i\omega t}\Big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+i\omega\int f(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt\).
Since \(f\to0\) at \(\pm\infty\) and \(|e^{-i\omega t}|=1\), the bracket is 0. So \(\boxed{\mathcal F\{f'\}=i\omega F(\omega)}\).
Spot the square: \(-t^2+2t-1=-(t-1)^2\). So the function is \((t-1)e^{-(t-1)^2}=g(t-1)\) with \(g(t)=te^{-t^2}\).
\(\mathcal F\{e^{-t^2}\}=\sqrt\pi e^{-\omega^2/4}\). Note that \(te^{-t^2}=-\frac12\frac{d}{dt}e^{-t^2}\), so by part (i): \(G=-\frac12\,i\omega\sqrt\pi e^{-\omega^2/4}\).
Time shift by 1: \(\boxed{\mathcal F=-\frac{i\sqrt\pi}{2}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega^2/4}e^{-i\omega}}\)
More worked properties Ex 5.10: \(\mathcal F\{2u(t)e^{-t}+u(t)e^{-2t}\}=\frac{2}{1+i\omega}+\frac1{2+i\omega}\).
Ex 5.15: \(3e^{-it}\) on \([-5,5]\) is \(3e^{-it}\) times a box with \(\alpha=5\), so \(F=\frac{6\sin5(\omega+1)}{\omega+1}\).
Ex 5.17 / Tut 2(i): \(\mathcal F\{4e^{-|t|}-5e^{-3|t+2|}\}=\frac{8}{1+\omega^2}-e^{2i\omega}\frac{30}{9+\omega^2}\).
Ex 5.34: \(\frac1{(9+i\omega)^2}=\frac1{9+i\omega}\cdot\frac1{9+i\omega}\), so \(f=(u e^{-9t})*(ue^{-9t})=te^{-9t}u(t)\).
Ex 5.43 (duality): \(\mathcal F\{\frac{2\sin t}{t}\}=2\pi\) for \(|\omega|<1\), \(\pi\) at \(|\omega|=1\), and 0 otherwise.

5.5 Inverse transforms (Exercise 5.45)

Reshape \(F\) until it matches a table entry, then undo the properties

\(\frac{2025}{5-i\omega}\). Since \(\mathcal F\{e^{at}u(-t)\}=\frac{1}{a-i\omega}\), the answer is \(2025e^{5t}u(-t)\).
\(\frac{\sin\omega\cos\omega}{\omega}=\frac14\cdot\frac{2\sin2\omega}{\omega}\), so the answer is \(\frac14\) on \(|t|\le2\) and 0 elsewhere.
\(e^{-\omega^2/12}\): Gaussian with \(4\alpha=12\), so \(\alpha=3\). Answer: \(\sqrt{\frac3\pi}e^{-3t^2}\).
\(e^{20i\omega}=e^{-i\omega(-20)}\), so the answer is \(\delta(t+20)\).
\(\frac{1}{\omega^2+3}=\frac1{2\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{2\sqrt3}{3+\omega^2}\), so the answer is \(\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}e^{-\sqrt3|t|}\).
\(\delta(\omega)\): \(\frac1{2\pi}\int\delta(\omega)e^{i\omega t}d\omega=\frac1{2\pi}\).
\(\frac1{(\omega+2)^2+9}\) is \(\frac16\cdot\frac{6}{9+\omega^2}\) shifted to \(\omega+2\), so the answer is \(\frac16e^{-2it}e^{-3|t|}\).
\(\frac{e^{-5i\omega}}{i\omega}\): since \(\mathcal F\{\frac12\operatorname{sgn}t\}=\frac1{i\omega}\), delaying by 5 gives \(\frac12\operatorname{sgn}(t-5)\).
\(\frac{2i\omega}{3+i\omega}=2-\frac{6}{3+i\omega}\), so the answer is \(2\delta(t)-6e^{-3t}u(t)\).
\(\frac{1}{15+8i\omega-\omega^2}=\frac1{(3+i\omega)(5+i\omega)}=\frac12\left(\frac1{3+i\omega}-\frac1{5+i\omega}\right)\), so the answer is \(\frac12(e^{-3t}-e^{-5t})u(t)\).

5.6 Even and odd functions, sine and cosine transforms

Half-line problems \((x\ge0)\) use \(F_s\) or \(F_c\)

Even f\(\mathcal F\{f\}=2\int_0^\infty f\cos\omega t\,dt\), which is real and even.
\(F_c(\omega)=\int_0^\infty f\cos\omega t\,dt\)
\(f=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty F_c\cos\omega t\,d\omega\)
Odd f\(\mathcal F\{f\}=-2i\int_0^\infty f\sin\omega t\,dt\), which is imaginary and odd.
\(F_s(\omega)=\int_0^\infty f\sin\omega t\,dt\)
\(f=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty F_s\sin\omega t\,d\omega\)
Ex 5.53 · f = e^{−at}\(F_c=\frac{a}{a^2+\omega^2}\), \(F_s=\frac{\omega}{a^2+\omega^2}\).
Tutorial 6: box \(|t|<1\) gives \(F=\frac{2\sin\omega}\omega\). Inverting at \(t=0\): \(1=\frac1{2\pi}\int\frac{2\sin\omega}{\omega}d\omega\), so \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}xdx=\frac\pi2\).

\(\theta_t=k\theta_{xx}\) gives \(\Theta_t=-k\omega^2\Theta\), so \(\Theta=\Theta(\omega,0)e^{-k\omega^2t}\).
\(\Theta(\omega,0)=\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\frac\pi a}e^{-\omega^2/4a}\), so \(\Theta=\sqrt{\frac\pi a}e^{-\omega^2\left(\frac1{4a}+kt\right)}\).

2025 exam Q3(b): infinite strip, Fourier transform in x (12 marks)

\(U_{xx}+U_{yy}=0\) on \(0<y<1\), \(U(x,0)=0\), \(U(x,1)=e^{-x^2}\).
Transform in \(x\): \(-\omega^2\hat U+\hat U_{yy}=0\), so \(\hat U=A\cosh\omega y+B\sinh\omega y\).
\(\hat U(\omega,0)=0\Rightarrow A=0\). \(\hat U(\omega,1)=\sqrt\pi e^{-\omega^2/4}\Rightarrow B=\frac{\sqrt\pi e^{-\omega^2/4}}{\sinh\omega}\).
\(\hat U=\sqrt\pi e^{-\omega^2/4}\dfrac{\sinh\omega y}{\sinh\omega}\)
Invert (the integrand is even in \(\omega\)):\[U=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-\omega^2/4}\frac{\sinh\omega y}{\sinh\omega}\cos\omega x\,d\omega\]
